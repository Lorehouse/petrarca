✦ Guided Voice Dump

Talk through what you know — 2-5 minutes per topic
Pick one or more topics below. Hit record on your phone, go for a walk or sit comfortably, and talk through the prompts. Don't try to be comprehensive — just say what comes to mind. Tangents are welcome. The goal is to capture the texture of what you know, not a perfect answer.
Prompt 1
Homer and the Epics
You rated this "know deeply" — let's capture what that means
~3-5 min
"Tell me about Homer and the Iliad and Odyssey — what do you know, what stands out to you, what connections do you see?"
  • What have you actually read? Translations, adaptations, excerpts?
  • What do you find most striking or memorable about the stories?
  • How do you think Homer connects to the rest of Greek culture — drama, philosophy, art?
  • Do you know anything about the "Homeric Question" — how were these texts created and transmitted?
  • Any modern works (novels, films, adaptations) that shaped your understanding?
Don't worry about covering every sub-question. Follow what's interesting to you.
Prompt 2
Plato — What Do You Actually Know?
You rated this "know the basics" but noted the calibration problem — let's find out what your basics are
~3-5 min
"Walk me through what you know about Plato — his life, his ideas, his works, his influence."
  • Can you place him in time and context? Who came before, who came after?
  • What do you know about the Theory of Forms? The Allegory of the Cave?
  • Have you read any of his dialogues? Which ones, what do you remember?
  • What's the relationship between Socrates and Plato? Where does one end and the other begin?
  • How has Plato influenced later thought — Christianity, the Renaissance, modern philosophy?
It's fine to say "I'm not sure about this part" — that's useful data too.
Prompt 3
The Persian Wars — Your Vivid Picture
You've read Iggulden's novels. What stuck? What's vivid and what's fuzzy?
~3-5 min
"Tell me the story of the Persian Wars as you understand it — what happened, why it mattered, what you remember most vividly."
  • What triggered the conflict? Why did Persia invade?
  • Walk through the key events — Marathon, Thermopylae, Salamis, Plataea
  • Which people do you remember? Themistocles, Leonidas, Xerxes, Darius?
  • What parts are vivid from the novels? What parts are fuzzy or you're unsure about?
  • Do you know what was happening in Sicily at the same time? (Battle of Himera, Carthage)
The novels gave you a vivid experience — we want to capture what's still active vs what's become latent.
Prompt 4
Greek Drama — Plays, Playwrights, and Why It Matters
You rated this "could explain" — one of your stronger areas
~2-3 min
"What do you know about Greek theater — who, what, why, and what have you actually read or seen?"
  • Can you characterize each of the three tragedians? What makes them different?
  • What plays do you know? Which ones have you read?
  • What about comedy — Aristophanes?
  • Why was drama a civic/religious event, not just entertainment?
Prompt 5
Connections and Gaps
The most interesting part — what links do you see, and where do you feel lost?
~3-5 min
"Looking at ancient Greece as a whole — what are the connections that excite you most, and where do you feel the biggest gaps?"
  • What cross-connections have you noticed? (e.g., Salamis and Himera in 480 BC)
  • Where do you feel like you have strong anchors that you could build from?
  • What topics came up in the assessment where you thought "I really want to know more about that"?
  • If you could have a one-hour conversation with an expert about one aspect of ancient Greece, what would you ask about?
  • How does your knowledge of Greece connect to other things you know — Rome, the Renaissance, modern politics, literature?
This is the most open-ended prompt. Follow your curiosity.
