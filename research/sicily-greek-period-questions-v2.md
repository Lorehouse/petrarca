# Sicily Greek Period — Hand-Crafted Level 1 Questions

*These are the skeleton facts. If you know these, everything else has hooks to attach to.*

## Colonization (~734 BC)

**Q1** [name→concept] What is the name of the first Greek colony on Sicily?
**A:** Naxos — founded by settlers from Chalcis around 734 BC on the east coast. (Syracuse was founded the same year by Corinthians.)
**Grading:** name correct / wrong
**Anchors:** Same year as Syracuse. About 250 years before Himera.

**Q2** [concept→date] When did Greek colonization of Sicily begin?
**A:** Around 734 BC, when Chalcidians founded Naxos and Corinthians founded Syracuse. This was part of the great wave of Greek colonization across the Mediterranean.
**Grading:** exact year / right decade (730s) / right century (8th c BC) / missed
**Anchors:** About 250 years before the Battle of Himera.

**Q3** [identity] Which Greek city founded Syracuse?
**A:** Corinth — not Athens, not Sparta. Corinth dominated western colonization. The settlers chose the island of Ortygia for its defensible harbors and freshwater spring (Arethusa).
**Grading:** correct / wrong

## Akragas (~580 BC)

**Q4** [name→concept] What is the modern name of the Greek colony Akragas?
**A:** Agrigento — on Sicily's south coast. Founded 580 BC. Famous for its spectacular Greek temples (the Valley of the Temples still stands) and extraordinary grain wealth.
**Grading:** correct / wrong
**Anchors:** Founded about 150 years after Syracuse. About 100 years before Himera.

**Q5** [concept→date] When was Akragas founded?
**A:** Around 580 BC. It grew fabulously wealthy from grain exports — Empedocles said its citizens "built as if they would live forever and feasted as if they would die tomorrow."
**Grading:** exact / decade / century / missed
**Anchors:** 150 years after Syracuse (734). 100 years before Himera (480).

## Battle of Himera (480 BC)

**Q6** [name→concept] What was the Battle of Himera?
**A:** A decisive Greek victory over Carthage on Sicily's north coast in 480 BC. Gelon, tyrant of Syracuse, crushed a massive Carthaginian invasion. This established Syracuse as the dominant power in the western Greek world.
**Grading:** know it was Greeks vs Carthage in Sicily / wrong

**Q7** [concept→date] When was the Battle of Himera?
**A:** 480 BC — tradition says the very same day as the Battle of Salamis in mainland Greece. The Greek world survived a coordinated two-front attack from Persia (east) and Carthage (west) simultaneously. Gelon used the spoils to build the Temple of Athena in Syracuse — its Doric columns still stand inside the cathedral.
**Grading:** exact year / right decade / right century (5th c BC) / missed
**Anchors:** Same year as Salamis (Persian Wars). About 250 years after colonization. About 70 years before the Athenian expedition.

**Q8** [identity] Who won the Battle of Himera?
**A:** Gelon, tyrant of Syracuse. He had seized Syracuse from Gela in 485 BC. After Himera, he was the most powerful Greek ruler anywhere — and used the spoils and slave labor to transform Syracuse into a metropolis.
**Grading:** correct (Gelon / Syracuse) / wrong

## Athenian Expedition (415-413 BC)

**Q9** [name→concept] What was the Athenian expedition to Syracuse?
**A:** Athens sent a massive fleet and army to conquer Syracuse in 415 BC during the Peloponnesian War. It ended in total catastrophe — the entire force was destroyed by 413 BC, ~40,000 men killed or enslaved. It marked the beginning of Athens' decline.
**Grading:** know Athens attacked Syracuse and lost / wrong

**Q10** [concept→date] When was the Athenian expedition to Syracuse?
**A:** 415–413 BC. Athens was at war with Sparta (Peloponnesian War) and made the catastrophic decision to open a second front in Sicily. The disaster weakened Athens so severely that it eventually lost the war to Sparta.
**Grading:** exact years / right decade (410s) / right century (5th c BC) / missed
**Anchors:** 65 years after Himera (480). About 10 years before Dionysius I takes power.

## Destruction of Akragas / Rise of Dionysius I (406-405 BC)

**Q11** [event→consequence] What happened to Akragas in 406 BC?
**A:** Carthage sacked and destroyed Akragas — one of the richest Greek cities in the world. The catastrophe terrified the Syracusans and directly led to Dionysius I seizing power in Syracuse the following year (405 BC).
**Grading:** know Carthage destroyed it / wrong
**Anchors:** About 10 years after the Athenian expedition. The year before Dionysius I takes power.

**Q12** [identity] Who was Dionysius I?
**A:** Tyrant of Syracuse (ruled 405–367 BC) — the most powerful ruler in Greek history at that time. He rose to power after Carthage sacked Akragas, built massive fortifications, waged four wars against Carthage, and controlled most of Sicily and southern Italy.
**Grading:** know he was tyrant of Syracuse / wrong

**Q13** [concept→date] When did Dionysius I come to power?
**A:** 405 BC — immediately after Carthage destroyed Akragas (406 BC). He used the crisis and Syracusan panic to seize power. He then ruled for 38 years until his death in 367 BC (reportedly from celebrating a drama prize too hard).
**Grading:** exact / decade (400s) / century (early 4th c BC) / missed
**Anchors:** Right after Akragas fell (406). About 75 years after Himera. 38 years before his death.

## Plato and Syracuse

**Q14** [connection] What connects Plato to Syracuse?
**A:** Plato visited Syracuse three times (c. 388, 367, 361 BC), hoping to create a philosopher-king. His first visit to Dionysius I's court ended badly — tradition says he was sold into slavery. His later visits to Dionysius II also failed. The experiences deeply shaped his political philosophy, including The Republic.
**Grading:** know Plato visited Syracuse / wrong

## Archimedes and the Siege of Syracuse (212 BC)

**Q15** [identity] Who was Archimedes?
**A:** Syracusan mathematician and inventor (~287–212 BC). One of the greatest scientists of antiquity. Studied in Alexandria, then returned to Syracuse. Famous for principles of buoyancy, calculating pi, and the lever. Defended Syracuse against Roman siege with ingenious war machines.
**Grading:** know he was from Syracuse + mathematician / wrong

**Q16** [event] How did Archimedes die?
**A:** Killed by a Roman soldier in 212 BC when Syracuse finally fell after a two-year siege. His war machines — catapults, giant cranes that lifted ships, possibly focused mirrors — had held off the Roman navy. The Roman general Marcellus reportedly wanted him alive but a soldier killed him anyway.
**Grading:** know killed during Roman siege of Syracuse / wrong
**Anchors:** Part of the Second Punic War (Rome vs Hannibal). About 190 years after Dionysius I. About 30 years after Sicily became Roman.

## Sicily Becomes Roman (241 BC)

**Q17** [concept→date] When did Sicily become part of the Roman Empire?
**A:** 241 BC, at the end of the First Punic War (Rome vs Carthage). Sicily became Rome's very first overseas province — the template for how Rome would govern all its future conquests. This ended 500 years of Greek independence on the island.
**Grading:** exact / decade / century (3rd c BC) / missed
**Anchors:** About 240 years after Greek colonization. About 30 years before Archimedes was killed.

## Sequence Check

**Q18** [ordering] Put in chronological order: Battle of Himera, Greek colonization begins, Athenian expedition to Syracuse, Dionysius I takes power
**A:** Colonization (734 BC) → Himera (480 BC) → Athenian expedition (415 BC) → Dionysius I (405 BC)
**Grading:** all correct / 3 of 4 / wrong

**Q19** [ordering] Put in order: Akragas destroyed by Carthage, Archimedes killed, Sicily becomes Roman province, Siege of Syracuse
**A:** Akragas destroyed (406 BC) → Sicily becomes Roman (241 BC) → Siege of Syracuse & Archimedes killed (212 BC)
Note: The siege of Syracuse IS when Archimedes was killed — this is a trick question if you separate them!
**Grading:** all correct / mostly right / wrong
