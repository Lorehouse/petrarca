✦ The Problem

What We Can't Answer Today

Petrarca can say "you know 35 of 70 Sicily nodes at the engaged level." It cannot say "you knew 20 a month ago and your connectivity score has doubled." The fundamental measurement question — is the system actually working? — remains unanswered.

Across sessions 39–60, substantial infrastructure was built: FSRS-6 scheduling, voice capture pipelines, knowledge profiles with transcript chunking, a statistics dashboard, card provenance tracking. All of it measures current state. None measures change over time.

The Core Gap

The April 5 deep-dive proposed a 5-phase plan. Phase 5 (knowledge profiles) was partially implemented because it was immediately useful for prompt quality. Phases 1–4 — the actual baseline sweep and longitudinal tracking — were never started. The most foundational piece, "record a baseline sweep NOW," was explicitly marked as Phase 1, do-this-first.

✦ Theory

Theoretical Foundations

The proposal draws on five well-established research traditions, each contributing a distinct lens on how to measure whether someone is actually learning.

1. Expert-Novice Knowledge Structures

Chi, Feltovich & Glaser (1981) established the foundational finding: experts categorize by deep principles, novices by surface features. The difference isn't knowledge amount — it's knowledge organization. Experts have richly interconnected schemas; novices store disconnected facts.

This was synthesized by the NRC's How People Learn (2000): "It is not simply general abilities, such as memory or intelligence, nor is it the use of general strategies that differentiate experts from novices. Instead, experts have acquired extensive knowledge that affects what they notice and how they organize, represent, and interpret information."

Key Implication

Petrarca should track not just what the user knows but how their knowledge is organized. A user who knows 50 facts about Sicily but can't connect them to larger patterns is still at a novice stage. The curriculum node structure already captures organizational principles — the gap is in measuring whether the user's mental model matches.

2. Free Recall as Assessment

Free recall — asking someone "tell me everything you know about X" — is one of the most powerful assessment methods in cognitive psychology because it reveals organization, not just content.

The research hierarchy is well-established:

  • Free recall (open-ended) → reveals both relational and item-specific processing
  • Cued recall (prompted) → reveals primarily item-specific processing
  • Recognition (multiple choice) → reveals only familiarity

Experts recall in larger, more organized chunks. They spontaneously articulate connections between items. Novices list isolated facts in random order. Schneider, Korkel & Weinert (1989) showed that domain knowledge predicted recall better than IQ — knowledge organization trumps general ability.

This directly validates Petrarca's voice elicitation: voice dumps are a direct implementation of free recall protocols. The system could analyze not just what's mentioned but recall order, clustering, causal language, and cross-references.

3. Pathfinder Networks: The North Star Metric

Goldsmith, Johnson & Acton (1991) validated Pathfinder networks as knowledge assessment tools. The similarity between a student's concept network and the instructor's network correlated with exam performance at r = .74 — a remarkably strong predictor.

Why This Matters for Petrarca

The curriculum graph IS the expert network. Each domain has ~50-80 nodes with prerequisite edges, entity links, and temporal relationships. The gap between the learner's demonstrated network and the curriculum graph, measured by structural similarity, is the single most informative metric of learning. The trajectory of edge_overlap from 0.0 toward 1.0 is the primary growth signal.

Three comparison metrics from Pathfinder research:

  • PRX (Proximity Correlation) — Pearson correlation on raw proximity ratings between all concept pairs
  • GTD (Graph-Theoretic Distance) — Correlation of shortest-path distances between all node pairs
  • PFC (Pathfinder Closeness) — Jaccard-like measure of shared neighborhoods per node

4. Fuzzy-Trace Theory: Why Types of Knowledge Decay Differently

Brainerd & Reyna's Fuzzy-Trace Theory (FTT) distinguishes two memory traces:

Verbatim Traces

  • Specific facts, dates, names, sequences
  • Decay quickly (days to a week)
  • Need frequent refreshment
  • Easier to relearn once decayed
  • E.g., "Battle of Salamis was 480 BCE"

Gist Traces

  • Frameworks, causal chains, arguments
  • Persist much longer (weeks to months)
  • Reactivated by a single encounter
  • Grow stronger with integration
  • E.g., "Greek colonization was driven by trade"

The 2025 MINERVA2 integration (Chang, Johns & Brainerd, Psychological Review) is the first computational operationalization of FTT — gist as distributional semantic vectors, verbatim as holographic word-form vectors. Currently FSRS treats all items identically. FTT says they shouldn't be.

5. The Testing Effect and Spreading Activation

Karpicke & Blunt (2011) showed retrieval practice outperforms even concept mapping, even on concept-map tests. The mechanism: retrieval activates and strengthens the entire semantic network around a concept, not just the target memory.

The elaborative retrieval hypothesis (Carpenter, 2009): testing one concept strengthens memory for related, untested concepts — as long as they're well-integrated. This means Petrarca's emphasis on connections (temporal hooks, cross-domain links) means reviewing one node should strengthen related nodes through spreading activation.

Anderson, Bjork & Bjork (1994) found retrieval-induced forgetting — but Chan et al. (2006) showed this reverses for well-integrated knowledge with delay. Petrarca's connected knowledge structure provides natural protection.

Key FSRS-6 Detail

FSRS-6 (21 parameters, 3,872 GitHub stars) tracks memory through the DSR model: Stability (interval at which R drops to 90%), Difficulty (1–10 scale), Retrievability via power function R(t,S) = (1 + factor * t/S)^(-w20).

Critical limitation: FSRS treats each item in isolation with binary recall. It has no concept of knowledge type, semantic relationships, or partial knowledge. The Difficulty parameter adapts organically, but can't distinguish a date from a framework. Per-deck optimization showed up to 16.9% RMSE reduction — the mechanism for type differentiation.

No new user interaction required. Built entirely from existing data. The system already records knowledge level transitions, review scores, and timestamps — it just isn't plotting them.

1B. Network Density Over Time

Use existing curriculum prerequisite edges + knowledge states to compute Goldsmith's edge overlap metric. Store as a time series — compute on dashboard load and snapshot weekly via cron.

import networkx as nx

def compute_network_metrics(domain_id: str, conn) -> dict:
    """Compute learner vs expert network similarity from existing data."""
    curriculum = load_curriculum(domain_id)
    states = {r[0]: r[1] for r in conn.execute(
        "SELECT node_id, knowledge FROM knowledge_items WHERE domain_id = ?",
        (domain_id,)
    ).fetchall()}

    expert_G = nx.DiGraph()
    learner_G = nx.DiGraph()
    for node in curriculum['nodes']:
        expert_G.add_node(node['id'])
        for prereq in node.get('prerequisites', []):
            expert_G.add_edge(prereq, node['id'])
        if states.get(node['id'], 'unknown') != 'unknown':
            learner_G.add_node(node['id'])
            for prereq in node.get('prerequisites', []):
                if states.get(prereq, 'unknown') != 'unknown':
                    learner_G.add_edge(prereq, node['id'])

    e_edges = set(expert_G.edges())
    l_edges = set(learner_G.edges())
    overlap = len(e_edges & l_edges) / len(e_edges) if e_edges else 0

    return {
        'node_coverage': len(learner_G.nodes()) / len(expert_G.nodes()),
        'edge_overlap': overlap,  # THIS is the Goldsmith C metric — r=.74
        'density': nx.density(learner_G) if len(learner_G) > 1 else 0,
    }
CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS network_metrics_log (
    id INTEGER PRIMARY KEY AUTOINCREMENT,
    domain_id TEXT NOT NULL,
    node_coverage REAL NOT NULL,
    edge_overlap REAL NOT NULL,
    density REAL NOT NULL,
    nodes_known INTEGER NOT NULL,
    nodes_total INTEGER NOT NULL,
    computed_at INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT (unixepoch())
);

1C. Review Performance Trend

Already have the data: knowledge_items has last_score, stability_days, due_at, last_reviewed_at. Three analyses:

  • Average stability per domain over time — are items getting easier to retain?
  • Distribution shift — % knew vs % partly vs % missed per week
  • FSRS calibration — compute retrievability at review time: R = (1 + factor * elapsed/stability)^(-w20), compare to actual score. Validates whether FSRS is well-calibrated. Important: use only post-session-60 data (the scheduling drift fix invalidated earlier data).

1D. Growth Visualization Page

Standalone HTML page at /knowledge/growth, following the existing pattern (like knowledge_atlas.html). D3.js line charts:

  • Coverage timeline — % curriculum known per domain over time. Multiple lines, one per domain.
  • Edge overlap trajectory — the Goldsmith C metric over time. The primary growth signal.
  • Review performance — weekly knew/partly/missed distribution.
  • Stability growth — average stability per domain (items getting easier to retain = learning working).
Framing Principle (Michel Thomas)

Show what has been learned (filled area growing) rather than what remains (empty area shrinking). "Your empire of knowledge is growing" beats "you still have 466 gaps." Positive framing matters enormously.

Tier 2 Voice Knowledge Sweeps

The centerpiece of the proposal. A "knowledge sweep" is a periodic (monthly) domain-wide voice assessment — 3 minutes of free recall scored against the full curriculum. Unlike per-node elicitation (which tests one concept), a sweep assesses the big picture.

Why This Works

From the research:

  • Free recall reveals knowledge organization, not just content (Tulving; Zemla & Austerweil)
  • Chronological prompting activates temporal scaffolding — the factual backbone we're building
  • 3 minutes is enough to reveal structure without exhausting the speaker
  • The same prompt can be repeated monthly to track growth
  • No existing system does repeated domain-level voice free recall as a longitudinal growth metric. This is genuinely novel.

The Protocol

Step 1: Prompt

"Tell me the history of Sicily — everything you can remember, in roughly chronological order. Speak for about 3 minutes."

Or for a focused sweep:

"Walk me through the timeline of ancient Greece from the Dark Ages to Alexander. What are the key periods, events, and people?"

Step 2: Transcribe

Soniox API (already integrated).

Step 3: Score via LLM

For each curriculum node: Was it mentioned? Were facts correct? Were connections articulated? Use Opus via claude -p (batch task, accuracy matters, cost doesn't).

Step 4: Compute Five Metrics

MetricMeasuresHow Computed
CoverageBreadth — how many nodes mentionednodes mentioned / total nodes
AccuracyCorrectness — FActScore-stylecorrect facts / total facts stated
ConnectivityStructure — connections articulatedconnections stated / possible connections between mentioned nodes
OrganizationExpert-like recall patternsChronological/thematic clustering vs. random order (0.0–1.0)
DepthLevel of understanding (Kintsch)Presence of causal language, perspective-taking, counterfactuals, cross-domain refs

Step 5: Compare to Last Sweep

Compute deltas across all metrics. Link each sweep to its predecessor for delta tracking.

Tier 3 Type-Differentiated Scheduling

Currently FSRS uses uniform parameters for all items. Fuzzy-Trace Theory says verbatim traces (dates, names) decay faster than gist traces (frameworks, causal chains). The key_facts already have type (date/event/person/connection/significance), so the mechanism is in place.

Option A: Separate desired_retention per Type

# Trivial to implement — just different FSRS retention targets
TYPE_RETENTION = {
    'date':       0.85,    # Higher target → more frequent review
    'event':      0.82,
    'person':     0.82,
    'connection': 0.75,    # Lower target → less review (gist persists)
    'significance': 0.75,
}

Option B: Duolingo Half-Life Regression

Replace FSRS per-item with HLR: log(h) = θ · x where the feature vector includes knowledge type. HLR naturally learns different decay rates per type. Improved retention by 12% at Duolingo. But requires 50+ reviews per type to train reliably.

Option C: Type-Differentiated Stability Multipliers

TYPE_STABILITY = {
    # Verbatim — decays faster, needs more frequent review
    'date':       {'initial': 1.0, 'knew_mult': 2.0, 'partly_mult': 1.3},
    'event':      {'initial': 1.5, 'knew_mult': 2.2, 'partly_mult': 1.4},
    'person':     {'initial': 1.5, 'knew_mult': 2.2, 'partly_mult': 1.4},
    # Gist — persists longer, bigger jumps when demonstrated
    'connection': {'initial': 3.0, 'knew_mult': 3.0, 'partly_mult': 1.8},
    'significance': {'initial': 3.0, 'knew_mult': 3.0, 'partly_mult': 1.8},
}
Recommendation

Start with Option A (trivial to implement via py-fsrs's per-item retention target). Run for 2 months, then evaluate. Option B (HLR) is more principled but needs data. Option C is an intermediate step if you're not yet using py-fsrs natively.

Natural Spacing Credit

When reading an article that maps to curriculum nodes, those nodes get a passive "re-encounter." This is more effective than artificial SRS because of contextual variation (different articles = different encoding contexts). Proposed: apply a small stability bump (×1.3) to nodes when they appear in articles being read. Track whether article-reinforced nodes show higher retention than review-only nodes.

✦ Context

Knowledge Profiles & the Digital Twin

Session 56 implemented the knowledge profile system — the first concrete step toward making LLM prompts aware of the learner. Here's what exists and what it enables for growth measurement.

What's Built

  • 786 transcript chunks with embeddings (limbic.amygdala)
  • 5 domain portraits synthesized from all voice data per domain
  • LEARNER CONTEXT injected into all 6 LLM templates (question gen, ML cards, enrichment, follow-ups, entity research, Hamarquizen)
  • Dual retrieval: relational links (chunk → node) + semantic search (embedding similarity)

How Profiles Connect to Growth Measurement

The profile system creates the observability layer that growth measurement needs:

Growth MetricProfile Data That Feeds It
CoverageChunk-node links tell which nodes the user has discussed. More links over time = growing coverage.
DepthChunk types (captured_fact vs. interesting vs. wondering) evolve. More "interesting" and "connection" chunks = deeper processing.
ConnectivityCross-domain entity links in transcript chunks. Number of entity-bridge chunks is a connectivity proxy.
OrganizationDomain portraits capture the user's organizing framework. Comparing portraits over time shows framework evolution.

The "Digital Twin" Vision

The aspiration (from the April 6 deep analysis): voice elicitation data should build toward a "limited digital twin of my brain."

What a Digital Twin Would Know

  • "Stian finds the Alexander-Aristotle relationship fascinating"
  • "Stian confuses Hiero I and Archimedes chronology"
  • "When thinking about Byzantium, organizes around Constantinople as a city, not emperors"
  • "Strong on Arab Sicily, weak on Norman succession"

What the System Currently Knows

  • knowledge_state: "engaged"
  • confidence: 0.45
  • last_score: "partly"
  • review_count: 3

That's the entire user model for a topic. Hours of voice data reduced to two numbers.

Two Modes of Elicitation

A critical insight from the deep analysis: voice elicitation serves fundamentally different purposes depending on temporal distance.

Distant Recall

  • Topics studied over time through multiple sources
  • Reveals what matters to the learner
  • Personal salience mapping
  • "Interesting" field is gold — original thinking

Fresh Recall

  • Chapter just read, podcast just heard
  • Rich in specific facts
  • Without processing, only gists survive
  • Retention prevention — anti-forgetting

The system should tag elicitations by mode (the UI already distinguishes chapter_recall vs. curriculum recall) and weight the data differently. Knowledge sweeps are explicitly a distant recall task, designed to reveal organization and growth.

✦ Novelty

What Doesn't Exist Anywhere Else

After extensive literature search across all 7 research documents: no existing system tracks a reader's knowledge state across multiple books and articles over months, using the combination of reading behavior, structured review, and free recall that Petrarca proposes.

The combination of:

  • Curriculum-aligned scoring of free voice recall
  • Longitudinal comparison across sweeps
  • Network structure analysis (not just fact counting)
  • Type-differentiated scheduling based on Fuzzy-Trace Theory

...does not exist in the literature. The closest systems:

  • ALEKS/KST — uses binary mastery, not graduated understanding
  • GENCAT — uses LLMs but for testing, not self-reported knowledge mapping
  • SPARFA — tracks knowledge over time but requires structured Q&A, not reading
  • Pathfinder — validated at r=.74 but never applied to voice transcript data
  • SNAFU — analyzes recall structure but for category fluency, not domain knowledge

Pathfinder network comparison has been validated but never applied to voice transcript data. This is publishable research.
