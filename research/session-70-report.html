Surprises

The Council of Nicaea Hallucination

Gemini proposed Q160538 for “Council of Nicaea” with confidence 1.0, confidently reasoning about “the First Ecumenical Council of 325 AD.”

Q160538 is actually Gian Lorenzo Bernini, the Italian sculptor. Pure hallucination.

Root cause: Wikidata search for “Council of Nicaea” returned only the disambiguation page (Q232572). With only one useless candidate, the LLM tried to generate the “right” QID from memory — and got it spectacularly wrong.

Resolution: validate_chosen_qid rejected the pick (not in candidate set). The disambiguation-page-only rescue then tried “First Council of Nicaea” and recovered the correct Q133331.

Takeaway: The “LLM never generates QIDs” invariant is load-bearing. This is the second documented hallucination incident (the first was me hallucinating 5/7 QIDs during PR 1 test fixture construction).

Design Choices Made

Decisions I made autonomously overnight, with reasoning
  1. Two-pass deterministic before LLM. Pass 1 resolves the easy cases for free ($0). Pass 2 uses those as coherence anchors to sharpen confidences on harder cases. LLM only touches items that can't be resolved deterministically — saves ~70% of LLM calls.
  2. Gemini Flash over Claude for disambiguation. 1–2s per call vs 5–15s. $0.05 total vs ~$15. For picking one from a short list with context, Flash quality is sufficient — 91% pick rate, 0.3% hallucination rate.
  3. QIDs from API only, never from LLM. The validate_chosen_qid guard rejects any LLM-proposed QID not in the candidate set. Proved load-bearing by the Council of Nicaea incident.
  4. Supersede-on-write, not delete-and-replace. Every resolution write chains via superseded_by. Full audit history preserved through deterministic → LLM → rescue → manual pipeline. Admin UI shows latest via MAX(created_at).
  5. Dedup safety classifier. Auto-merge only obvious cases: _person suffix, known aliases, identical names. Flag uncertain pairs (ancient_greece ↔ greece, abbasid_caliphate ↔ arab_caliphates) for human review rather than risk conflation.
  6. Schema deployed ahead of backfill. Additive-only ALTERs are zero-risk. Deploying early let me test endpoints and verify the migration was idempotent before data existed. Backfill itself is deferred to your explicit approval since it writes to production.
On naming: Throughout the overnight work I referred to the production server as “alif” — that’s the SSH alias for the Hetzner VM. Both Petrarca and the Alif Arabic project share the same machine. I’ll say “the server” or “Hetzner” going forward to avoid confusion.
