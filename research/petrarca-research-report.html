Personal Knowledge Tracking
for the Lifelong Reader

Research Directions & Measurement Framework

The Petrarca Project — April 2026

Petrarca is a reading companion that models what a reader knows about complex historical topics across books, articles, and personal notes—then uses that model to surface novel content, maintain knowledge through spaced review, and track genuine understanding over months. This report synthesizes 50+ internal research documents, a comprehensive literature survey, and proposes a measurement framework grounded in cognitive science. We identify specific hypotheses, experiment designs, potential collaborators, and novel contributions to the fields of learning analytics, knowledge tracing, and reading science.
Section I

Executive Summary

No existing system tracks a reader's knowledge state across multiple books and articles over months, using the combination of reading behavior, structured review, and free recall that Petrarca proposes. This has been confirmed through an extensive literature survey covering knowledge tracing (BKT, DKT, SPARFA), intelligent tutoring systems, adaptive reading platforms, and open learner models.

Core Thesis

The goal is not "move 466 nodes from unknown to anchored." It is: build a network where the reader can narrate any 5-minute stretch of Mediterranean history, connecting events across domains, explaining causation, and placing new reading into the existing framework. The curriculum provides the scaffold. Books provide encoding. Review maintains connections. Voice free recall is both the best exercise and the best measurement.

What Makes This Novel

  • Curriculum-based knowledge organization — 9 overlapping curricula (719 nodes) serve as expert concept maps against which reader knowledge is compared
  • Claim-level novelty detection — articles decomposed into atomic claims, embedded, and compared against the reader's knowledge ledger
  • Cross-source knowledge integration — books, articles, voice notes, and review all feed the same knowledge model
  • Voice free recall as primary assessment — spoken narratives analyzed for coverage, accuracy, connectedness, and situation-model depth
  • Natural spacing through reading — new articles that touch on previously-learned concepts create organic spaced retrieval

Data Flow Architecture

Two parallel knowledge loops feed the same curriculum scaffold:

  • Loop A (Articles): Twitter/Readwise → Gemini extraction → claims + embeddings → curriculum mapping (0.65 cosine) → feed ranking by novelty → reader with paragraph dimming
  • Loop B (Books): Kindle sync / manual add → book research agent → chapter→node mapping → knowledge_items → microlearning quizzes → FSRS scheduling

The curriculum is the bridge. Both articles and books map to the same 719 nodes. Knowledge states are updated from both paths, and voice elicitation provides a third independent assessment channel.

Section III

Research Foundations

Hooks, Not Facts

"Done" = able to PLACE new knowledge. Having enough framework that new information can be assimilated. — Design Vision, Petrarca (2026)

The system optimizes for building and maintaining hooks—anchor points of understanding that make future reading productive—rather than optimizing for recall of individual facts. This aligns with expert-novice research showing the critical difference is knowledge organization, not amount.

Expert vs. Novice Knowledge Structures

Chi, Feltovich & Glaser (1981) established that experts categorize by deep principles while novices categorize by surface features. The difference isn't primarily knowledge amount—it's knowledge organization. Experts have richly interconnected schemas organized around core principles.

Validated by Research

Schneider, Korkel & Weinert (1989) showed that domain expertise predicted recall better than IQ—low-IQ soccer experts outperformed high-IQ soccer novices. Knowledge organization trumps general ability for comprehension and recall.

Free Recall vs. Cued Recall

Karpicke & Blunt (2011, Science) demonstrated retrieval practice outperformed concept mapping for learning. The key mechanism: retrieval doesn't just strengthen individual traces—it activates the entire semantic network. Free recall specifically promotes both relational AND item-specific processing, while cued recall promotes only item-specific processing.

Fuzzy-Trace Theory

Brainerd & Reyna's framework distinguishes gist traces (essential meaning, frameworks) from verbatim traces (exact words, dates). Gist traces are far more durable. Retention strategy should reinforce gist occasionally and re-connect details to gist when encountered in new contexts.

The Mnemonic Medium

Matuschak & Nielsen's Quantum Country data: after 95 minutes of review, ~95% retention across 2+ weeks. Matuschak's 2023 pivot was crucial: "What seems like a problem of forgetting is sometimes a problem of reading comprehension—never having understood in the first place."

Historical Thinking

Sam Wineburg (2001) showed historians read fundamentally differently—the "sourcing heuristic" (who created this, when, for what audience?) is the touchstone distinguishing expert from novice practice. Peter Seixas (2013) codified six historical thinking concepts as assessment benchmarks: significance, evidence, continuity/change, cause/consequence, perspectives, and ethical dimensions.

CMU's KLI Framework

Koedinger, Corbett & Perfetti define three types of knowledge components: Facts (direct associations), Skills (nonverbal mappings), and Principles (mappings with verbal rationale). Each curriculum node should be decomposed into all three for effective assessment.

Desirable Difficulties

Bjork & Bjork (2011): conditions that slow apparent learning—struggle, uncertainty, interleaving—tend to optimize long-term retention. Rohrer & Taylor (2007): interleaved practice produced 43% better delayed performance than blocked practice.

The Pathfinder Insight

Key Insight

Goldsmith, Johnson & Acton (1991) showed that similarity between a student's knowledge network and an expert's network correlated with exam performance at r = .74. The Petrarca curriculum already is the expert concept map. The gap between the reader's demonstrated knowledge structure (from voice recall) and the curriculum graph is the learning target. Convergence over time is the best measure of whether the system works.

Section V

Hypotheses

Component-Level

H-C1
FSRS predictions match actual recall
After 20+ reviewed knowledge_items accumulate, compare FSRS-predicted retrievability to actual quiz scores. If FSRS predicts 80% retrievability and users score knew 80% of the time, the model is calibrated.
Measure: Correlation between predicted retrievability and observed knew_pct across review sessions.
H-C2
Key_facts with temporal hooks have higher recall
Among reviewed key_facts, those tagged with temporal context (dates, cross-references to known events) should show higher knew rates, controlling for review_count.
Measure: Tag each key_fact as temporal/non-temporal. Compare recall rates after 2+ reviews.
H-C3
Voice elaboration predicts future recall better than buttons
For nodes where the user both pressed a button AND gave a voice elaboration, which better predicts the score on the next review? If voice is dramatically more predictive, button assessments are noise.
Measure: Compare predictive validity (AUC) of voice-scored depth vs. button score for next-review outcome.

Integration-Level

H-I1
Article reading boosts curriculum node review scores
When the article→curriculum link fires AND the user reads the article, mapped nodes should show higher knew rates on their next review than equally-due nodes that weren't article-reinforced. Tests "natural spacing."
Measure: Compare review scores for article-reinforced vs. non-reinforced nodes at matched due dates.
H-I2
Cross-domain entity encounters create nexus effects
When a shared entity is encountered in one curriculum and then reviewed in another, the second review should show higher scores than if the entity were only reviewed in one domain.
Measure: Compare scores for shared-entity nodes with multi-domain encounters vs. single-domain.

Vision-Level

H-V1
Free recall coverage grows monotonically with system use
Monthly voice sweeps should show increasing node coverage for actively-studied domains. If coverage plateaus or drops while the user is still reading, the system's maintenance mechanism is failing.
Measure: Monthly coverage % tracked over 6+ months. Fit growth curve.
H-V2
Knowledge structure density increases faster than knowledge amount
In monthly voice sweeps, the ratio of cross-node connections to total nodes mentioned should increase. Users don't just accumulate more facts—they connect them more densely. This IS the "hooks not facts" thesis made testable.
Measure: Connections-per-node ratio tracked monthly. Should show superlinear growth.
H-V3
The system correctly identifies the knowledge frontier
Nodes rated "mentioned" should cluster at the edge of what the user can articulate in free recall. "Anchored" nodes should be elaborated. "Unknown" should be absent. If this holds, knowledge_states reflect reality.
Measure: Correlation between knowledge_state level and elaboration depth in voice transcript.
H-V4
Second books produce faster knowledge integration
When reading a second book about a domain already studied, the rate at which new knowledge_items reach "engaged" should be faster than for the first book. The existing scaffold accelerates learning.
Measure: Time-to-engaged for book 1 vs. book 2+ in same domain.
Section VI

Voice Assessment Innovations

Key Insight

The voice timeline exercise isn't just a measurement tool—it's the best possible review format for relational knowledge. Three minutes of spoken free recall exercises exactly the connections that make historical knowledge stick, and produces a richer knowledge state signal than any number of quiz cards.

Scoring Dimensions

Each voice response is LLM-scored along multiple dimensions:

  • Coverage — How many curriculum nodes were mentioned?
  • Accuracy — Were facts and dates correct?
  • Connectedness — How many causal/temporal links between nodes?
  • Depth — Surface form, textbase, or situation model (Kintsch)?
  • Cross-domain reach — Connections to other curricula?
  • Coherence — Narrative flow and organization (Coh-Metrix inspired)
Section VII

Experiment Designs

N=1 Case Study: Knowledge Growth Over 6 Months

Single-subject research designs are well-established in educational psychology. The A-B-A withdrawal design and multiple baseline design across domains are particularly applicable.

Design: Multiple Baseline Across Domains

  • Phase A (baseline): Monthly voice sweep for Sicily, Greece, and Rome—no review intervention
  • Phase B (intervention): Introduce review system for Sicily first, then Greece, then Rome at staggered intervals
  • Measure: Free recall coverage % and connectedness for each domain
  • Expected result: Coverage increases for each domain only after intervention begins, demonstrating causality
  • Publication venue: Journal of Applied Behavior Analysis, Journal of Educational Psychology

Design: Natural Spacing Validation

  • Track which curriculum nodes are "naturally spaced" (article→curriculum mapping fired + article read)
  • Compare FSRS stability growth for naturally-spaced vs. only-reviewed nodes
  • Expected: naturally-spaced nodes show 30%+ higher stability growth
  • Publication venue: Learning & Instruction, LAK conference

Design: Voice vs. Button Assessment Comparison

  • For 100 knowledge_items, collect both button scores and 30-second voice elaborations
  • Compare predictive validity for next-review outcome
  • Expected: voice elaboration depth is a stronger predictor (AUC > 0.80) than button press (AUC ~ 0.65)
  • Publication venue: AIED, EDM conferences

Design: Pathfinder Map Distance Tracking

  • Monthly: extract reader concept map from voice transcript
  • Compute Pathfinder C-metric (shared links) against curriculum graph
  • Track convergence over 6-12 months
  • Compare across domains at different stages of learning
  • Publication venue: Journal of Educational Psychology, L@S

Section VIII-b

Multiple Document Comprehension

The research on reading across multiple texts provides the strongest theoretical validation for Petrarca's cross-source approach.

Key Finding

A 2024 experiment in Instructional Science (186 participants) found that multiple text reading produces significantly better long-term retention than rereading, with only slight decline between 1-day and 1-week tests. Rereading groups showed increasing (false) confidence; multiple-text groups maintained accurate metacognitive calibration.

The Documents Model (Perfetti, Rouet & Britt, 1999)

Multiple-document reading requires two additional representations beyond single-text comprehension: an Integrated Mental Model (unified understanding across sources) and an Intertext Model (who said what, and how sources relate). Without scaffolding, readers produce a "mush model" — merging information but losing source attribution. Petrarca's claim-level tracking with source metadata directly addresses this.

The Discrepancy-Induced Source Comprehension Model

Braasch & Bråten (2017): When sources disagree, readers actually build richer mental representations than when sources agree. Disagreement triggers more source attention, more text-switching, better memory for attribution. Implication: Petrarca should flag discrepancies between sources as enriching opportunities, not confusing errors.

Norwegian Connection: Bråten & Strømsø at UiO

Ivar Bråten (Emeritus, University of Oslo) led Norway's Research Council project on "Multiple-Documents Literacy" (2009–2012). His finding: readers' epistemic beliefs shape multi-text processing — those believing knowledge is complex produce cross-text elaborations; those believing knowledge is simple produce single-text paraphrases. Helge Strømsø (also UiO) identified three justification dimensions in multi-source reading: authority, personal justification, and multiple-source justification.

Practical Implications

  • Source attribution is cognitively valuable — not just organizational bookkeeping
  • Present complementary perspectives close together — contrasting views need temporal proximity for comparison in working memory
  • Scaffolding helps but not all types equally — visual mapping tools (comparison tables, entity networks) are most effective
  • Framing matters enormously — RESOLV model (Rouet & Britt, 2017) shows that context/purpose shapes processing more than text properties
Section VIII-c

Voice Assessment Technology

No existing automated speech scoring system handles open-ended content/knowledge assessment well. ETS SpeechRater, Pearson Versant, and others focus on language proficiency. This is the gap Petrarca would fill.

LLM-Based Scoring Is Ready

NYU Voice AI Study (2026)

Ipeirotis & Rizakos (NYU, March 2026) built scalable oral assessments using a "Council of LLMs" (Claude + Gemini + GPT-5). After deliberation: Krippendorff's α = 0.86 (above "good reliability" threshold). Cost: $0.42/exam vs ~$750 for human equivalent. 70% of students agreed it tested genuine understanding.

Key Tools Available

  • FActScore — Decomposes text into atomic claims, verifies against knowledge base. Open source.
  • SemScore — Single cosine similarity number between transcript and reference. Outperforms more complex metrics.
  • LLM-Rubric (Microsoft) — Calibrated multidimensional evaluation framework. Open source.

Vasquez Ferrer et al. (2026): Self-reported confidence (prompting the LLM for a 1–10 confidence score) achieved the lowest Expected Calibration Error (0.166) across all conditions, outperforming the 5× more expensive self-consistency method. One LLM call, not five.

Section VIII-d

N=1 Methodology & Personal Science

Petrarca is inherently a single-user system. The personal science movement and single-case experimental designs (SCEDs) provide rigorous frameworks for this.

Precedents for Self-Experimentation in Learning

  • Ebbinghaus (1880–1885) — One person's data founded the entire field of memory research
  • Piotr Wozniak — 40+ years of continuous personal learning data since 1982. Discovered the stabilization curve ~1 century after the forgetting curve
  • Michael Nielsen — 10,000+ Anki cards over 2.5 years. Average review interval now 1.2 years
  • Gwern Branwen — Gold standard for rigorous self-experimentation: blinding, randomization, MANOVA, raw data published
  • Matuschak — Quantum Country: several hundred users with 1+ month demonstrated retention

Best Design: Multiple Baseline Across Domains

Multiple baseline designs are preferred for learning interventions because knowledge, once learned, cannot be "unlearned" as reversal designs require. For Petrarca: introduce the review system for Sicily first, then Greece, then Rome at staggered intervals. Coverage increases for each domain only after intervention begins, demonstrating causality. This is the standard in 69% of published SCEDs.
