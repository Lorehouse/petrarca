# Structured Curricula for Self-Education Knowledge Mapping

*Research date: 2026-03-31*

Goal: Survey freely available, high-quality structured curricula that could serve as the basis for Petrarca's knowledge mapping system. Focus on history, classical civilization, and related humanities — matching the user's existing curricula (Ancient Greece, Rome, Sicily, Byzantine/Islamic) and interest in becoming "at least as educated as an educated high schooler" in these areas.

---

## Executive Summary

The best sources for structured curriculum data, ranked by usefulness for knowledge mapping:

| Source | Node estimate | Date coverage | Freely available? | Structure quality |
|--------|-------------|---------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| **AP World History: Modern** | ~66 topics | c. 1200–present | Full CED PDF free | Excellent — numbered topics with learning objectives |
| **AP European History** | ~88 topics | c. 1450–present | Full CED PDF free | Excellent — same format |
| **OpenStax World History (2 vols)** | ~130 sections | Prehistory–present | CC-BY textbook | Excellent — hierarchical chapters/sections |
| **AQA A-Level History** | ~22 topic options | Medieval–20th C | Full spec PDF free | Good — but pick-your-own, not sequential |
| **OCR Classical Civilisation** | ~10 module options | Ancient Greece/Rome | Spec PDF free | Excellent for classics specifically |
| **IB History** | ~12 world topics + 5 prescribed | 750–present | Guide behind paywall | Good structure but less accessible |
| **AP Art History** | 250 works / 10 areas | 30,000 BCE–present | Full CED PDF free | Good for cultural knowledge |
| **Yale Open Courses** | ~70 lectures (3 courses) | Bronze Age–1000 CE | Free video + text | Good but lecture-shaped, not curriculum-shaped |
| **MIT OCW** | ~60 sessions (2 courses) | Bronze Age–5th C CE | Free full materials | Good for ancient world specifically |
| **Norwegian vgs (LK20)** | ~28 competence aims | General (all periods) | Free on udir.no | Too abstract — aims, not content |
| **Khan Academy** | mirrors AP structure | c. 1200–present | Free but JS-rendered | Mirrors AP WH; hard to extract programmatically |

**Recommendation**: AP World History + AP European History + OpenStax Vol 1 (for ancient/pre-1200 coverage) + OCR Classical Civilisation gives the most comprehensive, well-structured foundation. These four together cover prehistory through the present, with deep classical coverage, and all have freely downloadable specification documents.

- **Date range**: 30,000 BCE to present
- **Structure**: 10 content areas, 250 required works of art

## 4. IB History (International Baccalaureate)

### Overview
- **Date range**: 750 CE to present (world topics); ancient through modern (depth studies)
- **Structure**: 5 prescribed subjects + 12 world history topics + 4 HL regional depth studies
- **Guide**: Behind IB paywall (not freely downloadable), but structure is well-documented
- **New curriculum**: Launching August 2026 with restructured components

### Current Structure (through 2027 exams)

**Prescribed Subjects (choose 1 of 5)**
1. Military Leaders
2. Conquest and Its Impact
3. The Move to Global War
4. Rights and Protest
5. Conflict and Intervention

**World History Topics (choose 2 of 12)**
1. Society and Economy (750–1400)
2. Causes and Effects of Wars (750–1500)
3. Dynasties and Rulers (750–1500)
4. Societies in Transition (1400–1700)
5. Early Modern States (1450–1789)
6. Causes and Effects of Early Modern Wars (1500–1750)
7. Origins, Development, and Impact of Industrialization (1750–2005)
8. Independence Movements (1800–2000)
9. Emergence and Development of Democratic States (1848–2000)
10. Authoritarian States (20th Century)
11. Causes and Effects of 20th-Century Wars
12. The Cold War: Superpower Tensions and Rivalries (20th Century)

**HL Depth Studies (choose 1 of 4 regions)**
- History of Africa and the Middle East
- History of the Americas
- History of Asia and Oceania
- History of Europe

### New Curriculum (first teaching Aug 2026)
- Paired Case Studies (5 options, replacing prescribed subjects) — source-based
- Global Themes (4 options, replacing world topics) — comparative
- Regional In-Depth Topics (same 4 regions) — HL only
- Four core concepts: Cause & Consequence, Continuity & Change, Perspectives, Significance

### Assessment for Knowledge Mapping
- **Strengths**: Genuinely global. The thematic approach (e.g., "Dynasties and Rulers 750–1500") is interesting for cross-cultural comparison.
- **Weakness**: The full guide is paywalled. The pick-your-own structure means there's no single "IB History" path. Topics 1-3 are the only ones reaching back to the medieval period.
- **Node estimate**: Each world topic has ~12 specific case studies = ~144 nodes if all 12 topics mapped. Realistically, 50-70 nodes for the 2-3 topics most relevant.

- **Node estimate**: ~130 sections. If each section maps to 3-5 key concepts, ~400-650 knowledge nodes total.
- **Critical value**: This is the only high-quality free source that covers prehistory through 1200 CE in structured form.

## 6. Khan Academy

### Overview
- **Structure**: Multiple courses — World History (general), World History AP, World History Origins, World History 1750
- **Access**: Free, but content loads dynamically via JavaScript — cannot be easily scraped or downloaded as static data
- **URL**: https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/world-history

### Course Structure
The AP-aligned course (OER Project: World History AP) mirrors the AP World History: Modern CED exactly — same 9 units, same topic structure. Not independently useful beyond what the AP CED already provides.

The "Origins" course (WHP Origins) covers earlier periods in ~7 units:
1. Introduction to World History
2. Early Humans (250,000 BP–3000 BCE)
3. Cities, Societies, and Empires (6000 BCE–700 CE)
4. Regional developments
5. The First Global Age
6. The Long Nineteenth Century (1750–1914)
7. The Great Convergence and Divergence (1880–future)

### Assessment for Knowledge Mapping
- **Strengths**: Free, high-quality content. Video + article format could supplement curriculum nodes.
- **Weakness**: No downloadable structured data. The API is not public. JS-rendered pages are hard to extract. For curriculum structure, the AP CED is better. For pre-1200 content, OpenStax is better structured.
- **Node estimate**: Mirrors AP WH (~66 topics) for the AP course. Origins course adds ~30-40 additional topics for pre-1200.

- **Focus**: Ancient Greece and Rome — literature, art, politics, religion, society
- **Structure**: 3 components with options
- **Specification**: https://www.ocr.org.uk/Images/315133-specification-accredited-a-level-classical-civilisation-h408.pdf

### Components

**Component 1: The World of the Hero (compulsory, 40%)**
- Homer's *Iliad* or *Odyssey*
- Virgil's *Aeneid*

**Component 2: Culture and the Arts (choose 1, 30%)**
- 2.1 Greek Theatre (Sophocles, Euripides, Aristophanes)
- 2.2 Imperial Image (Augustus to Nero — sculpture, architecture, coinage)
- 2.3 Invention of the Barbarian (Greek vs non-Greek identity)
- 2.4 Greek Art (sculpture, vase painting, architecture)

**Component 3: Beliefs and Ideas (choose 1, 30%)**
- 3.1 Greek Religion (ritual, sanctuaries, festivals, afterlife)
- 3.2 Love and Relationships (Sappho, Ovid, Plato's *Symposium*)
- 3.3 Politics of the Late Republic (Cicero, Caesar, political crisis)
- 3.4 Democracy and the Athenians (institutions, ideology, critics)

### Assessment for Knowledge Mapping
- **Strengths**: DIRECTLY aligned with user's classical interests. Deep engagement with primary sources (Homer, Virgil, Herodotus, Thucydides, Plato). The most relevant single curriculum for classical civilization. Freely downloadable specification.
- **Weakness**: Narrow — only Greece and Rome. Not world history.
- **Node estimate**: If all options mapped: ~80-100 nodes covering literary, artistic, political, religious, and social aspects of classical civilization.

## 11. Norwegian Videregående History (LK20 HIS01-03)

### Overview
- **Date range**: All of history (no period restriction)
- **Structure**: Competence aims for vg2 (year 12) and vg3 (year 13)
- **Specification**: https://www.udir.no/lk20/his01-03?lang=eng (free, English translation available)

9. Explore communication and cultural encounters

### Competence Aims after vg3 (17 aims)
1. Reflect on how interpretations of the past are affected by present understanding
2. Reflect on how the past is used by different stakeholders
3. Explore using research questions and assessing materials
4. Explore a historic figure and discuss their ideas/options/choices

### Assessment for Knowledge Mapping
- **Strengths**: Competency-based rather than content-based — interesting pedagogical model. Freely available in English.
- **Weakness**: TOO ABSTRACT for knowledge mapping. These are skills and reflective aims, not content specifications. "Explain ideas and ideologies behind political revolutions" doesn't specify WHICH revolutions. A teacher fills in the content; the curriculum doesn't prescribe it. Essentially unusable for generating curriculum nodes.
- **Node estimate**: 28 aims, but each is too vague to be a knowledge node.

### The Gap
The biggest gap is **300 BCE to 1200 CE in a single structured curriculum**. No AP course covers this period. The user's existing Ancient Greece/Rome/Sicily/Byzantine+Islamic curricula partially fill it, but there's no comprehensive framework equivalent to what AP provides for 1200+. OpenStax Vol 1 Units 2-3 and Yale's Freedman course are the best sources for this crucial millennium.

## Recommended Curriculum Stack for Implementation

### Priority 1: Immediate value (well-structured, freely downloadable, numbered topics)
1. **AP World History: Modern CED** — 66 topics, c. 1200–present, global
2. **AP European History CED** — 88 topics, c. 1450–present, European depth
3. **OpenStax World History Vol 1** — 62 sections, prehistory–1500 (fills the ancient gap)

### Priority 2: Classical depth (matches existing curricula)
4. **OCR Classical Civilisation spec** — ~80 nodes on Greek/Roman literature, art, politics, religion
5. **Yale Open Courses** — Kagan (Greece), Freedman (Early Middle Ages), Kleiner (Roman Architecture)

### Priority 3: Supplementary
6. **AQA A-Level 1A: The Crusades** — fills the 1071–1204 gap specifically
7. **AP Art History** — 250 canonical works for cultural literacy
8. **OpenStax World History Vol 2** — 63 sections, 1400–present (mostly overlaps AP courses)

### Total estimated nodes
- AP WH topics: 66
- AP Euro topics: 88
- OpenStax Vol 1 sections: 62
- OCR Classical Civ: 80
- Yale lecture topics: 70
- AQA Crusades: 35
- **TOTAL: ~400 curriculum nodes at the topic level**
- Expanded to learning objectives: ~1,500–2,000 fine-grained knowledge nodes

### Implementation approach
The AP CEDs are the most immediately usable — they have numbered topics, each with 3-8 "Essential Knowledge" statements that are essentially atomic claims. These could be directly converted to Petrarca curriculum nodes using the existing `curriculum.py` infrastructure. OpenStax sections could be similarly decomposed. The existing Opus-based curriculum generation pipeline could enrich these with dates, prereqs, and cross-curriculum entities.
