How People Learn

Cognitive science foundations — what actually works for retaining complex knowledge

Cognitive Free recall beats cued recall for deep knowledge
Karpicke & Blunt (2011, Science): retrieval practice outperformed concept mapping. Free recall promotes both relational AND item-specific processing. Cued recall (quiz questions) promotes only item-specific processing. Implication: spoken narratives are the best review format for historical knowledge, not quiz cards.
Cognitive Gist memory lasts months; verbatim memory lasts days
Brainerd & Reyna's Fuzzy-Trace Theory: "the Pirenne thesis reshaped medieval historiography" (gist) persists for months. "The book was published in 1937" (verbatim) decays in weeks. The FSRS should use different stability parameters: dates at 14 days, causal frameworks at 45 days.
Cognitive Desirable difficulties: spacing, interleaving, retrieval
Bjork & Bjork (2011): conditions that slow apparent learning optimize long-term retention. Interleaved practice produced 43% better delayed performance (Rohrer & Taylor 2007). Review should interleave across domains, not block by topic.
Cognitive Expert knowledge is organized, not just bigger
Chi, Feltovich & Glaser (1981): experts categorize by deep principles; novices by surface features. Schneider et al. (1989): domain expertise predicted recall better than IQ. Track knowledge structure, not just knowledge amount.
Cognitive Tests cause learning, not just measure it
Roediger & Karpicke (2006): practice tests → 50% more retention than repeated study. The "forward effect": testing material A improves learning of material B. Even failed retrieval attempts help (Arnold & McDermott 2013). Every quiz card is a learning event.
Cognitive Comprehension before memory
Matuschak's 2023 pivot: "What seems like a problem of forgetting is sometimes a problem of never having understood." Quantum Country: after 95 minutes of review, ~95% retention across 2+ weeks. Ensure understanding at encoding time, not just schedule review.
Cognitive Self-explanation is powerful: g=0.55 across 64 studies
Meta-analysis of self-explanation effect. Voice note-taking produces higher conceptual understanding than typing (Kang et al. 2020). Production effect: speaking aloud improves memory 10-20%. Voice is the ideal capture and review modality.

How Historians Think

What "understanding history" actually means — beyond dates and names

History The sourcing heuristic distinguishes experts
Sam Wineburg (2001): historians immediately check attribution (who, when, for whom) before engaging with content. Students dive in directly. Historical thinking is "unnatural" — requires actively suppressing presentism. The system should eventually track whether users contextualize, not just recall.
History Seixas' Big Six assessment benchmarks
Historical significance, primary source evidence, continuity & change, cause & consequence, historical perspectives, ethical dimensions. Each has graduated benchmarks from naive to sophisticated. Voice recall can be scored against these dimensions, not just factual coverage.
History The AP Complexity Point
Awarded for explaining nuance, multiple causation, cross-period connections, qualifying arguments. This is the clearest operational definition of what distinguishes deep historical understanding from competent recall. The gold standard for "anchored" knowledge — can you demonstrate complexity?
History Epistemic progression: Copier → Subjectivist → Criterialist
Maggioni's three stances toward historical knowledge. Copiers treat sources as transparent windows. Subjectivists see all views as equally valid. Criterialists evaluate evidence using disciplinary criteria. Track epistemic sophistication, not just factual knowledge.
History Shemilt's big picture framework (4 levels)
From "timeline of events" to "multidimensional narrative with causal structure." Maps directly to Petrarca's knowledge levels: a timeline = mentioned; causal narrative = anchored. Use voice timeline tasks to assess which level the user has reached.

Reading Across Sources

What happens when you read multiple books and articles on the same topic

Core Validation

A 2024 experiment (186 participants, Instructional Science): multiple text reading produces significantly better long-term retention than rereading, with only slight decline between 1-day and 1-week tests. Rereading groups developed false confidence. Multiple-text groups maintained accurate metacognitive calibration. This validates Petrarca's entire approach.

Multi-doc The Documents Model (Perfetti, Rouet & Britt, 1999)
Multiple-document reading requires two representations beyond single-text: an Integrated Mental Model (unified understanding) and an Intertext Model (who said what, how sources relate). Without scaffolding, readers produce a "mush model" — losing source attribution. Petrarca's claim-level tracking with source metadata directly addresses this.
Multi-doc Disagreement triggers deeper processing
D-ISC model (Braasch & Bråten 2017): when sources disagree, readers build richer mental representations. More source attention, more text-switching, better memory for attribution. Flag discrepancies between books as enriching opportunities, not errors.
Multi-doc Epistemic beliefs shape everything
Bråten (UiO): readers believing knowledge is complex produce cross-text elaborations; those believing knowledge is simple produce single-text paraphrases. The "hooks" philosophy inherently treats knowledge as complex and interconnected.
Multi-doc Context/purpose shapes processing more than text
RESOLV model (Rouet & Britt 2017): same texts in a university-seminar context triggers dramatically more source comparison, conflict acknowledgment, and corroboration than personal context. UI framing matters — the review screen should activate "scholarly" processing standards.

Tools for Thought

What's been built before, what worked, what didn't

System Matuschak's mnemonic medium: 95% retention, 95 minutes
Quantum Country: 112 embedded prompts across 4 essays on quantum computing. After 1.5 hours of review, retention holds 9+ weeks. "Programmable attention" — SRS as "a cron for your mind." Salience prompts keep ideas top-of-mind, not just retrievable. Petrarca extends this to multiple sources, not one text.
System ALEKS: convergence in ~25 questions
Knowledge Space Theory (Doignon & Falmagne 1985): despite millions of possible knowledge states, ALEKS converges in ~25 adaptive questions. 4-5 million students annually. Proves you can assess a complex structured domain quickly with the right structure.
System GENCAT: generative adaptive testing with LLMs (Feb 2026)
Feng & Lan (UMass): Generative IRT predicts/generates student open-ended responses, not just correctness. DPO alignment with IRT parameters. 4.32% AUC improvement over traditional CAT. Closest existing system to Petrarca's voice assessment approach.
System No existing system does what Petrarca does
Extensive literature survey confirmed: no system tracks reader knowledge across multiple books and articles over months using reading behavior + structured review + free recall. Closest: ALEKS (single domain), Readwise (highlights only), iSTART (single text). The combination is genuinely novel.

Measuring Knowledge

How to know whether the system is actually working

The Pathfinder Insight

Goldsmith, Johnson & Acton (1991): similarity between a student's knowledge network and an expert's network correlates with exam performance at r = .74. The Petrarca curriculum already IS the expert concept map. The gap between the reader's demonstrated structure (from voice recall) and the curriculum graph is the learning target. Convergence over time is the single best measure.

Measure Kintsch's three levels of understanding
Surface form ("the book said…") → Textbase (correct propositions) → Situation model (causal reasoning, cross-source connections, counterfactuals). Detectable in voice transcripts via causal language, perspective-taking, counterfactuals. Quality score per node, not just binary "mentioned."
Measure CMU KLI: Facts / Skills / Principles
Koedinger, Corbett & Perfetti: three types of knowledge components. Facts (direct associations), Skills (nonverbal mappings — like sequencing events), Principles (verbal rationale — like "imperial overextension leads to decline"). key_facts only test facts. Need sequencing tasks (skills) and "why" questions (principles).
Measure SNAFU: semantic networks from free recall
Zemla & Austerweil (Memory & Cognition 2026): free recall data reveals memory organization structure. Quantifies clustering, connectivity, and organizational quality. Python tool, open source. Apply directly to voice transcripts to extract and quantify knowledge structure.
Measure FActScore: atomic claim verification
Decompose transcript into atomic claims, verify each against curriculum/knowledge base. Score = % of supported claims. Core (2024) filters trivially obvious claims to prevent score inflation. The right way to score factual accuracy of voice dumps.

Voice & NLP Technology

What's practically usable today for scoring spoken knowledge

Voice Soniox: multilingual streaming, $0.12/hr
60+ languages in a single model. Automatic code-switching (Norwegian↔English mid-sentence). Sub-200ms latency for streaming. Already integrated in Petrarca.
Voice NYU Voice AI: Council of LLMs (α=0.86)
Ipeirotis & Rizakos (March 2026): three LLMs independently score, then deliberate, then Claude synthesizes. Krippendorff's α = 0.86 after deliberation. $0.42/exam vs ~$750 human. The scoring architecture for voice sweeps.
Voice CoTAL prompting: 94.7% agreement with experts
Cohn et al. (2025): Evidence-Centered Design + Chain-of-Thought + rubric alignment. The LLM quotes student responses, ties to rubric items, pinpoints proficiency and difficulty. Best prompt engineering pattern for voice assessment.
Voice Self-reported confidence is cheapest calibration
Vasquez Ferrer et al. (2026): prompting the LLM for a 1-10 confidence score → lowest Expected Calibration Error (0.166). One LLM call, not five. 86% of predictions exceed 0.8 confidence. Use for selective human escalation.
Voice Practical scoring pipeline
Soniox (transcription) → spaCy NER (entity extraction) → key_facts comparison (coverage) → SemScore (embedding similarity to curriculum nodes) → Claude + rubric (holistic assessment: accuracy, coverage, depth, coherence, gaps) → update knowledge_states.

What to Build Next

Organized by "what problem does this solve for me as a reader?"

"Am I actually learning?"
Monthly voice sweep + Pathfinder map distance. Prompt: "Tell me the history of Sicily." Record 3 min. LLM scores against curriculum for coverage, accuracy, connectedness. Track monthly. The curriculum graph is the expert map. The gap between your recall structure and the curriculum is the learning target. This is both the best measurement AND the best exercise (Karpicke: free recall promotes relational processing).
"What should I review?"
FSRS differentiation by knowledge type. Dates (verbatim) get 14-day base stability. Connections and causal frameworks (gist) get 45-day. Based on Fuzzy-Trace Theory — these genuinely decay at different rates. Config change, not architecture change. Also: bootstrap knowledge_items for the 466 uncovered nodes so the review flywheel has material.
"What connects across what I'm reading?"
Entity profiles + cross-domain nexus visualization. Design exists (entity-profiles-design.md). 261 entities with 511 cross-curriculum links already in the database. The "Archimedes appears in Sicily + Greece + History of Science" view, with accumulated knowledge per entity. Also: flag when two books disagree — D-ISC research says disagreement triggers deeper processing.
"Is the system's model of me accurate?"
Voice vs button calibration. On 20% of review cards, offer "say more?" (15 sec voice). Compare LLM-scored elaboration depth with the button press. If correlation is low, the buttons are noise. Also: compare FSRS-predicted retrievability to actual quiz scores — if the model says 80% and you get 50%, recalibrate.
"How do I maintain knowledge over months?"
Natural spacing through reading. When you read an article that maps to curriculum nodes, those nodes get "naturally spaced" — a new contextual encounter that's intrinsically motivated. Track whether article-reinforced nodes show higher stability growth than review-only nodes. Also: Knowledge Health Score (composite monthly metric) so you can see the trend.
"What should I read next?"
Firehose curriculum + novelty-guided feed. Create at least one curriculum for tech/AI content so Mode A articles enter the knowledge maintenance loop. Currently the 9 curricula are all historical — tech reading has no scaffold. The 70% novelty curiosity zone already works for articles; connecting it to curriculum gaps would make it prescriptive.
"Can I recall the big picture?"
Domain narrative prompts + timeline construction. Voice formats: "Put these 5 events in order" (tests Skills, 60 sec), "Why did the Athenian expedition fail?" (tests Principles, 60 sec), "What was happening elsewhere when…?" (tests cross-domain connections, 45 sec). The Feynman test: "Explain Roger II to someone who knows nothing about him" (tests situation model, 90 sec). All generate richer signal than quiz cards.
"Where are my gaps?"
Discovery probes for 466 uncovered nodes. Use key_facts where available (367 nodes have them), curriculum descriptions for the rest. The curriculum scan screen exists but doesn't create knowledge_items at scale. Also: extract key_facts for the remaining 4 domains (352 nodes) — enables deterministic questions everywhere.
